First, an introduction

so many of you just got here, which means a lot of you are looking at this going "who is this woman and why is she calling me bestie." completely fair. let me start over properly.

six years ago i started single besties and became a comedian entirely by accident. i was not planning either one of those. that is genuinely the whole origin story.

but here is how i actually became the single bestie. i dated the wrong men. all of them. my favorite ex cheated on me with a man. i have been through baby mama drama, abuse, situationships that had no business lasting as long as they did... i swear i have been through it all, baby.

i am not proud of all of it. but it forced me to become this. and i would not trade her for anything.

since then i spent an entire year solo traveling, self employed, a bit of a digital nomad, mostly living out of a carry on and saying yes to things.

but the thing i have always been, before any of it, is a community builder. that is the part that stuck. so here is where this is actually going... i want us together in person. live events. comedy shows. group trips. the group chat is where it starts, not where it ends.

ok. now the news.

 

Part one

what's going on in my world.

we hit 28,000 on instagram this week. and i found out laying in bed next to my mom on a family trip. that was the moment. no launch party, no photoshoot. me, my mother, and a phone. #RichAuntieEnergy.

i am the only single person in my entire family, and at this point i have fully accepted the assignment. this summer alone i launched my first ever merch line, babycakes, named in honor of my sittu who just passed on... my brother had a baby last month (more on that later, it is a whole thing), and i turned my guest room into Camp Nina for my nieces and nephews while they were in town for summer vacation.

rich auntie energy is not a caption for me. it is a full time position, and it comes with a bunk bed situation.

 

Part two

what's going on in the single world.

the singles events are 90% women and everybody noticed. a clip went around this week about men not showing up to singles events, and the internet produced roughly ten thousand comments of feelings about it. the theory that kept coming up: the men doing fine on the apps have no reason to buy a ticket, and the ones who aren't assume a room won't go better than a screen. so nobody comes, and a hundred women stand around holding drink tickets.

but here is the part i actually care about. the events that did work in those comments were not mixers at all. they were the ones where you are doing something. a group hike. trivia. a class. anything where you rotate and talk to people because the activity makes you.

aka if you have been wanting to try that class, that run club, that pottery thing... go try it. you never know who you might meet.
